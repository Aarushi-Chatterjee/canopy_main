everyone's stuck in the same gap

Finding who to build it with is harder than the idea ever was.

People are scattered across LinkedIn DMs, Discord servers, university networks, and random Slack groups. There's no structured way to post a project, find complementary skills, form a team, and actually ship. The result: endless scrolling, half-started ideas, and brilliant people staying stuck.

a living ecosystem, three layers deep

Match. Sprint. Notebook. That's the whole loop.

Loose intent becomes shipped work through three deliberate steps: you match with intent, commit to a real deadline, and walk away with something you can point to.

Step 01

Match

grab a shovel, not a swipe

Build a profile with your domains, skills, and collaboration style, and get a generative avatar: a visual fingerprint of your building identity, not a photo. Browse Build Calls and people, and respond with a short note on what you'd bring. The poster actually reads it before choosing who joins, which is what makes the match feel human instead of automated.

Step 02

Sprint

the mingle becomes a team

Once a team forms, everyone enters a shared workspace: a starter repo, sample data, and a clear two-to-four week deadline. A living sprint clock keeps the gentle pressure of shared momentum on, so the rhythm stays building, checking in, adjusting: the kind of progress a month-long email chain never quite manages.

Step 03

Notebook

document the digging, not just the win

Ship a prototype, a dashboard, or a write-up, then log the process in the open. What worked, what failed, what is next. It grows the Library, grows your presence, and becomes the proof point that attracts the next teammate, funder, or challenge.

the mingle, in practice

This is the Problem Marketplace: real challenges posted by companies, NGOs, and labs, each with context, data, and a reward. Review each challenge with a tick or a cross: an honest read on which problems are worth your next sprint, never a blind match on the people behind them.
